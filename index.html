How I work

One loop, any environment.

Twenty years across products and categories taught me that the value isn't in the industry knowledge; it's in the process that produces it. This is the loop I run on everything, from a broken funnel to an app that doesn't exist yet.

↺ restart with what you learned

The fork that de-risked a sequel.* A large evergreen franchise earned most of its revenue from ads: hostage to fill rates, eCPMs and provider volatility. The sequel was entering development on an unproven business model.

  1. 0Listened to leadership's real fear: building the sequel on guesswork. Collected revenue telemetry and market signals; chest-based economies were at their prime.
  2. 1Structured the data: in-app revenue was smaller but predictable and tunable. Assumption drafted: the audience would accept a purchase-first economy.
  3. 2Confronted leadership and redefined success: not the spinoff's revenue, but a validated economy blueprint for the sequel.
  4. 3Hypothesis: fork the original codebase and redesign the core loop around chests, card copies and dual currency. Publish it in selected test markets in place of the original.
  5. 4Measured, iterated, then handed the validated playbook to the sequel team. Their business model was built on evidence, not guesses.

The product was never the fork. The product was the answer.

The subscription nobody could pay for.* LATAM families wanted kids' content subscriptions, but conversions said otherwise. The assumed blocker was price. Listening pointed somewhere else.

  1. 0Talked to the market before touching the pricing page: demand was real, and price sensitivity didn't explain the drop-off.
  2. 1Structured the funnel data: the audience existed, the payment rail didn't. Credit card penetration was the wall.
  3. 2Aligned the business on the reframe: the constraint was infrastructure, not demand.
  4. 3Hypothesis: carrier billing removes the wall. Negotiated a direct partnership with a major mobile carrier to charge subscriptions to the phone bill.
  5. 4Shipped, A/B tested pricing and flows, iterated. Subscriptions for families the industry considered unreachable.

The audience was always there. The rail wasn't.

Five years in, written off.* A licensed mobile product most would call finished. The easy read was decline; the loop said look closer.

  1. 0Listened to the telemetry and the user community instead of the conventional wisdom about mature products.
  2. 1Structured engagement decay by segment: the most committed users were still there. The content calendar wasn't serving them.
  3. 2Aligned on what growth means for a mature product: active users and revenue, without burning the loyal base.
  4. 3Hypotheses: restructure the content calendar, design seasonal events, segment the offers.
  5. 4Shipped and measured: active users grew on a product everyone had written off.

Mature doesn't mean finished.

Context

A hard paywall at the end of onboarding

Keel turns personal goals into short daily sessions with an AI coach. Nobody uses it without a plan: $12.99 a month, $29.99 a quarter, or $59.99 a year with a 7-day free trial. About 2,480 people a day reach the paywall, and 14.5% of them start a plan.

Step 1 · Observe

Where people leave, and why

The current shop takes two screens to reach a price. Screen one lists seven features and three buttons, but no price. Screen two offers three plans with three equal buttons and no default.

See the shop10,000
Reach the plans screen4,800
5,200 leave on screen 1, the biggest leak
Tap a subscribe button2,160
2,640 leave the plans screen
Start a plan1,447 · 14.5%
  • Nobody reads the features. 61% never scroll past the third card. People who leave stay a median of 4 seconds.
  • The first button confuses. "Connect" gets 9% of taps; the two "See plans" buttons get 39%.
  • The choice stalls. 38% of plans-screen leavers switch between cards first: hesitation, not rejection.
  • The price is a mystery. 4 of 6 interviewees: "I didn't know what it cost."
Step 2 · Assume and hypothesize

The path to the price is the problem, not the price

People don't leave because Keel costs too much. They leave because the price is two screens away, the value is never stated, and the final step asks them to choose between three equal buttons.

IF the two-step shop becomes one benefit-led screen with the yearly trial pre-selected, THEN start rate rises by at least 10%, BECAUSE one step disappears, a default replaces a three-way choice, value comes before price, and the risk is answered next to the button ("$0.00 today, reminder before it renews").

Why +10%, built from the funnel. If the new screen wins back a small share of the people we lose today, the lift lands here:

ScenarioScreen 1 won backPlans won backLift
Conservative1 in 401 in 20+9%
Expected1 in 251 in 12+14%
Optimistic1 in 151 in 8+22%

Risks named before launch.

  • Weaker trials. An easier trial can attract people who were never going to pay.
  • Plan mix shift. Monthly and quarterly become a detour behind "See all plans".
  • A bundled change. If it wins, we know the package works, not which part. A later test isolates the framing.
Step 3 · Design

One test arm, one framing held back, one plans screen

Who
New installs only

Nobody sees both versions. 50/50 split, checked daily for imbalance.

How many
17,000+ per arm

Enough to detect +10% on start rate (9,700 needed) and to judge paid users per view.

How long
14 days, read on day 24

Two full weekly cycles, then time for 7-day trials to convert. No early calls.

Segments
Agreed upfront

Platform, country tier, paid vs organic. Nothing added after the fact.

Why start rate can't decide alone. If trial to paid drops 5 points, it fully cancels a 10% gain in starts. So start rate gets the early read, and paid users per view makes the ship call.

Step 5 · Decide

Ship, keep a holdout, and test the fix

Weeks 1 to 2
Staged rollout

25%, then 50%, then 100%, watching the guardrails at each stage.

30 days
5% holdout

A small group keeps the old shop, to confirm revenue per view at day 30.

Next
Test 2B on top of 2A

The first-week timeline sets expectations for trial users. Primary metric: trial to paid.

Reverse if
Pre-agreed triggers

Paid users per view below control at 50%, or 24h cancellations above 25%.

The win wasn't the +15%. It was knowing which number to trust, and what to fix next.

Caveat: first payment revenue undervalues monthly plans, which renew every month. The real test of yearly plans is renewal at month 12; the holdout keeps that question open.

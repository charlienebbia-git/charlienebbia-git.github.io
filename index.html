How I work

One loop, any environment.

Nineteen years across genres taught me that the value isn't in the industry knowledge; it's in the process that produces it. This is the loop I run on everything, from a broken funnel to an app that doesn't exist yet.

↺ restart with what you learned

The fork that de-risked a sequel.* A game company's evergreen franchise (~10M monthly players) earned roughly 90% of its revenue from ads: hostage to fill rates, eCPMs, and provider volatility. The sequel was entering development on an unproven business model.

  1. 0Listened to leadership's real fear: building the sequel on guesswork. Collected revenue telemetry and market signals; chest-based economies were at their prime.
  2. 1Structured the data: IAP revenue was smaller but predictable and tunable. Assumption drafted: the audience would accept an IAP-first economy.
  3. 2Confronted leadership and redefined success: not the spinoff's revenue, but a validated economy blueprint for the sequel.
  4. 3Hypothesis: fork the original codebase, redesign the core loop around chests, card copies, and dual currency. Publish it in selected test markets in place of the original.
  5. 4Measured, iterated, then handed the validated playbook to the sequel team. Their business model was built on evidence, not guesses.

The product was never the fork. The product was the answer.

The subscription nobody could pay for.* LATAM families wanted kids' content subscriptions, but conversions said otherwise. The assumed blocker was price. Listening pointed somewhere else.

  1. 0Talked to the market before touching the pricing page: demand was real, and price sensitivity didn't explain the drop-off.
  2. 1Structured the funnel data: the audience existed, the payment rail didn't. Credit card penetration was the wall.
  3. 2Aligned the business on the reframe: the constraint was infrastructure, not demand.
  4. 3Hypothesis: carrier billing removes the wall. Negotiated a direct partnership with a major mobile carrier to charge subscriptions to the phone bill.
  5. 4Shipped, A/B tested pricing and flows, iterated. Subscriptions for families the industry considered unreachable.

The audience was always there. The rail wasn't.

Five years in, written off.* A licensed mobile RPG most would call finished. The easy read was decline; the loop said look closer.

  1. 0Listened to the telemetry and the player community instead of the conventional wisdom about mature titles.
  2. 1Structured engagement decay by segment: the spenders were still there. The calendar wasn't serving them.
  3. 2Aligned on what growth means for a mature title: MAU and revenue, without burning the loyal base.
  4. 3Hypotheses: restructure the LiveOps calendar, design seasonal events, segment the offers.
  5. 4Shipped and measured: MAU +9% on a title everyone had written off.

Mature doesn't mean finished.
